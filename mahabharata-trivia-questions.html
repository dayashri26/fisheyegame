10 Mahabharata Trivia Questions Every Fan Should Know

A short set of mahabharata trivia questions on Arjuna, Karna, Eklavya, and the fish-eye trial to test how well you really know the epic.

Few epics reward a sharp memory the way the Mahabharata does. Its eighteen parvas hold thousands of named characters, dozens of near-impossible feats, and a moral complexity that keeps scholars arguing centuries later. Whether you grew up hearing it from a grandparent or discovered it through television and comics, a good round of mahabharata trivia questions has a way of showing you how much you actually absorbed — and how much slipped past you. This list gathers ten questions built around the epic's most memorable figures and moments, each with a proper explanation rather than a bare one-word answer, so it works as much as a quick refresher as a hindu mythology trivia game for a group of friends.

Ten Questions to Test Your Knowledge

  1. Who refused to teach Eklavya archery, and why? Dronacharya turned Eklavya away because he had committed to teaching only the Kuru princes, and the tribal prince Eklavya was not among them. Undeterred, Eklavya built a clay image of Drona in the forest and trained before it with such devotion that he eventually surpassed even Arjuna in raw skill, a feat that later cost him his thumb when Drona claimed it as tuition.
  2. What was the Matsya-Vedh, and why did it matter at Draupadi's swayamvara? The Matsya-Vedh, or fish-eye trial, required a suitor to string a heavy bow and shoot an arrow through a small aperture to pierce the eye of a rotating fish figure mounted above — while aiming only at its reflection in a pool or vessel of oil below, never glancing at the target directly. Arjuna, disguised as a Brahmin during the Pandavas' exile, alone completed it, winning Draupadi's hand and setting off the chain of events that bound the two houses together in rivalry.
  3. Which warrior was cursed to forget his archery knowledge at the moment he needed it most? Karna, trained secretly by Parashurama who taught only Brahmins, was cursed to forget his most powerful mantras when he most needed them, after Parashurama discovered Karna's true identity as a Kshatriya through a small deception. That curse resurfaced fatally on the battlefield of Kurukshetra.
  4. Why were the Pandavas exiled for thirteen years rather than a shorter term? After losing a rigged game of dice to Duryodhana and Shakuni, Yudhishthira was bound by its terms to twelve years in forest exile followed by one year living incognito; if discovered during that final year, the exile would restart. The Pandavas met the condition, spending the last year at King Virata's court, where Arjuna's swayamvara-winning skill was later called upon again in disguise.
  5. How many parvas, or books, make up the Mahabharata? The epic is traditionally divided into eighteen parvas, ranging from the early Adi Parva covering the Kuru lineage to the concluding Svargarohana Parva describing the Pandavas' ascent to heaven. This structure is one of the most commonly tested facts in any mahabharata quiz, since the number eighteen also recurs meaningfully elsewhere in the story, including the eighteen-day span of the Kurukshetra war itself.
  6. Who is traditionally credited with composing the Mahabharata? The sage Vyasa, also known as Krishna Dvaipayana, is credited as the epic's author, and tradition holds that he dictated it to the god Ganesha, who agreed to write only on the condition that Vyasa never pause in his narration. Vyasa is also a character within his own story, appearing as the biological father of Dhritarashtra, Pandu, and Vidura.
  7. What made Dronacharya such a decisive figure in the training of the Kuru princes? Dronacharya was the royal preceptor who trained both the Pandavas and the Kauravas in the arts of war, with a particular gift for archery that made Arjuna his most accomplished pupil. His loyalty was famously divided between duty to his royal patrons and personal affection for his students, a tension that shaped his role, and his death, during the war.
  8. What does the word Mahabharata actually mean? The title translates roughly to the great tale of the Bharata dynasty, referring to the descendants of King Bharata from whom the Kuru and Pandava lines both claimed descent. The epic itself frequently reminds readers that it aims to contain nothing less than the whole of dharma, artha, and human experience within its scope.
  9. How was the outcome of Draupadi's swayamvara ultimately shared among the five brothers? When Arjuna won Draupadi through the fish-eye trial and brought her home, their mother Kunti unknowingly told her sons to share whatever they had brought equally, and out of respect for her word, Draupadi became the shared wife of all five Pandava brothers. This unusual arrangement, unique even within the epic, reflects the intense loyalty to elders and to spoken vows that runs through the entire narrative.
  10. What triggered the eighteen-day Kurukshetra war? Years of accumulated grievances, culminating in the failed peace negotiations after the Pandavas' exile and Duryodhana's refusal to return even five villages to his cousins, led both sides to muster their armies at Kurukshetra. It was on the eve of that war that Krishna delivered the counsel to Arjuna known as the Bhagavad Gita, addressing the archer's doubt before the fighting began.

Real mahabharata trivia questions are worth revisiting because the epic rewards close attention rather than surface familiarity — the same way the fish-eye trial rewarded focus on a reflection rather than the obvious target above it. Frequently Asked Questions

How many parvas are in the Mahabharata? The epic is traditionally divided into eighteen parvas, running from the Adi Parva to the concluding Svargarohana Parva. The number eighteen recurs elsewhere in the story too, most notably in the eighteen-day span of the Kurukshetra war.

Who is credited with writing the Mahabharata? The sage Vyasa, also known as Krishna Dvaipayana, is traditionally credited as its author, dictating the epic to the god Ganesha, who agreed to write only if Vyasa never paused his narration.

What is the Matsya-Vedh trial? It is the fish-eye trial from Draupadi's swayamvara, requiring a suitor to string a heavy bow and shoot a rotating fish figure's eye by aiming only at its reflection in a pool below. Arjuna, disguised as a Brahmin, was the only one who succeeded.

Why is the number eighteen significant in the Mahabharata? Eighteen recurs throughout the epic as a structural motif: it is the number of parvas that make up the text and also the number of days the Kurukshetra war lasted, making it one of the most commonly tested facts in any Mahabharata quiz.
