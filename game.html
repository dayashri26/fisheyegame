The Three Motions

Observe. Align. Strike.

The trial rewards stillness, not speed. Every draw comes down to three motions, repeated until your aim is true.

Observe

Never look at the fish itself. Find its reflection rippling in the pool below and fix your attention there alone.

Align

Move to aim, then hold. The longer you draw the string with a steady hand, the truer the shot — but the fish never stops turning.

Strike

Release the instant the reflection lines up. Hesitate and the moment passes; rush and the arrow goes wide.

A 5,000-Year-Old Trial, Retold

The Mahabharata is one of the great epics of ancient India — the story of a royal family torn apart by a struggle for the throne. Before that struggle begins, a lone archer named Arjuna must pass an impossible test: strike the eye of a spinning fish above a pool, aiming only at its reflection in the water below. Matsya-Vedh turns that single moment into a game.

Before You Draw

Frequently Asked

Is Matsya-Vedh free to play?
Yes. No download, no account, no payment — it runs directly in your browser, on desktop or mobile.
Do I need any special skill or archery knowledge?
No. The trial is easy to understand — move to aim, hold to draw, release to shoot — and gets harder as you climb levels, so anyone can start immediately.
Is this based on a real story?
Yes. It's drawn from a famous episode in the Mahabharata, one of India's great ancient epics, where the archer Arjuna strikes a spinning fish's eye by aiming only at its reflection.
Is my score saved?
Your personal best, level record, and achievements are saved in your own browser on this device, so they'll be waiting for you next time you visit.
Does it work well on mobile?
Yes — the game is fully playable with touch: tap and hold on the arena to draw, release to shoot.
